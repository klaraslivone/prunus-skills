# Expert and research

## Expert profile (phase 1)
Answer these and summarise in 5–8 lines:
- Which domains does the task cross? (usually 1 main + 1–2 supporting)
- Who would be the ideal expert? Role, practice, type of training.
- Which frameworks, models and terms do they use? (name them concretely)
- What are the most common beginner mistakes in the domain?
- How does the expert recognise a quality result? (criteria → later the skill's review)
- What are the ethical or safety limits of the domain (health, law, finance, children)?

## Research (phase 2)
Scale to complexity: a simple workflow 3–5 searches, a specialist domain 8–15.

**Source types – search across:**
1. Textbooks and standard literature of the domain
2. Talks, conferences, practitioner podcasts
3. Designer/practitioner diaries, case studies ("how we did it")
4. Research (studies, review articles)
5. Official documentation, standards, methodologies
6. Existing tools, templates and finished solutions (often the best "diff" between lay and expert practice)
7. Community experience (forums) – only as a supplement

**What to extract:**
- Principles and invariants (what must not be broken)
- Decision levers ordered from least to most invasive
- A taxonomy/catalogue (types of situations and how to handle them)
- What must be asked of the user at runtime
- A model output format and quality criteria
- Typical mistakes and how the skill will catch them

**Quality rules:**
- For every source, state whether it was verified (opened/searched) or cited from knowledge.
- Numbers and statistics always with a source and marked exact / estimate / lower bound.
- Where sources disagree, say so.
- Fewer sources used well beat a long list.
